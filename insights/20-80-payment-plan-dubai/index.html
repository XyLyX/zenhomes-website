The 20/80 Payment Plan: What to Check Before You Sign

A 20/80 payment plan lowers what you pay before handover, but it does not lower what you owe. Before you sign, test three things: the price, your exit, and how you will fund the 80%.

A 20/80 payment plan lowers what you pay before handover, but it does not lower what you owe. Before you sign, test three things: the price, your exit, and how you will fund the 80%.

A launch billed as the “best investment yet” tells you nothing about value. This guide focuses on Dubai off-plan purchases where 20% is paid before handover and 80% at completion.

Three Catches Behind the Headline

1. The Deferral May Be Priced In

Developers carry a cost when they wait for your money, and they can recover it through a higher price per square foot. The size of any premium varies, so measure it. Compare the launch price with a construction-linked plan, such as 60/40, and with recent sales of similar ready homes nearby.

2. Reselling Before Handover May Need More Cash

Developers typically require 30% to 40% of the price to be paid before a resale, though the figure varies. A resale also needs the developer's No Objection Certificate (NOC). On a strict 20/80 plan, you could need to pay another 10% to 20% early to qualify.

On an AED 2 million home, that is AED 200,000 to AED 400,000 on top of the AED 400,000 already paid. NOC fees generally range from AED 500 to AED 5,000 plus VAT. Your sale and purchase agreement (SPA) sets the actual terms.

3. The 80% May Not Be Fully Financeable

UAE Central Bank caps for a completed home, as summarised by Enness Global, include 80% for a resident expatriate's first home up to AED 5 million. For a second or investment property the cap is 60%. Off-plan lending is capped at 50%, and non-resident limits depend on the bank.

Check the Area and the Full Cost

Price the space you can use. A headline price per square foot may be based on total area, including balconies. For example, AED 1,800 per sq ft on total area becomes about AED 2,350 if only 77% of it is internal space. Compare like with like.

Rebuild the yield. Ask what any advertised return leaves out:

  • The 4% Dubai Land Department fee, plus registration and administration charges
  • Utility connections, deposits and any cooling charges
  • Furnishing, including any mandatory package
  • Holiday-home or property management fees
  • Annual service charges after handover, maintenance and vacant periods

A Four-Step Stress Test

  1. Compare ready sales. Use recorded transactions, not asking prices, for similar ready buildings nearby over the past three to six months. As a working rule, question any off-plan premium: you take construction risk and earn no rent while you wait.
  2. Audit your exit. Find the resale threshold, NOC fee and assignment conditions in the SPA.
  3. Count the supply. Check how many similar units are due from six months before to twelve months after your handover. A cluster can pressure rents and resale prices.
  4. Calculate net yield on total cost. Use conservative rent and your own occupancy assumption. The 92% below suits a cautious long-let example; short lets may be much lower.

Net yield = [(Annual rent × 0.92) − Service charges − Maintenance and management] ÷ [Price + DLD fee + Admin and utility setup + Furnishing]

This figure excludes mortgage interest. If you borrow, run the cash flow after loan payments too.

Five Questions to Ask Before Signing

  1. What are the internal and balcony areas, and what is the price per square foot on internal space alone?
  2. How much must be paid before a resale NOC is issued, and what does it cost?
  3. Which recorded sales of ready homes nearby support this price?
  4. Is the project registered, and what are its escrow account details? Who is the contractor, and how have past handovers compared with promised dates?
  5. What are the estimated service charges, and how is cooling billed?

The Zen Homes View

Do not buy a project for its payment plan. Buy only if the price stands up against recorded sales, the unit has qualities that are hard to replace, and you can fund handover even if the valuation falls. If those tests fail, walk away.
