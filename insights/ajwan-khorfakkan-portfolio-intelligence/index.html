Ajwan Khorfakkan: What Shurooq Is Building on Sharjah's East Coast.

A government-backed master developer, a coastline being repositioned as the UAE's next second-home market, and a per-building price spread wide enough to make "Ajwan pricing" close to meaningless as a single figure. A considered account of the developer, the destination, and where the exposure sits.

By Navvin S Bhalla · Zen Homes Advisory — Last updated September 27, 2026

Share Copied

Most East Coast product in the UAE is still sold on scenery. Ajwan Khorfakkan is being sold — and increasingly delivered — as institutional infrastructure: a wholly government-backed master developer, a phased construction programme now under a single main contractor, and a transaction market in the parent emirate that grew 64.3% in a single year. What follows separates the developer's own claims from what is independently verifiable, and sets out where the genuine exposure lies.

The Developer: Shurooq, Not a Private Sponsor

Ajwan Khorfakkan is developed by the Sharjah Investment and Development Authority (Shurooq), Sharjah's government investment vehicle, rather than a private or listed developer. Shurooq's own disclosed portfolio includes Maryam Island — an AED 4.5 billion (~$1.2bn), 460,000 sqm mixed-use waterfront development in Downtown Sharjah delivered with Eagle Hills Sharjah — and Sharjah Sustainable City, a net-zero-energy master-planned community built with Diamond Developers. Shurooq states its real estate portfolio has expanded at a Market claim 48.9% CAGR and represents over AED 7.2 billion in investments across 15 years of activity, with a reported 25% rise in foreign real estate investment into the emirate.

The distinction matters for underwriting: a sovereign-backed developer changes the delivery-risk profile relative to a single-project private sponsor, but it does not by itself establish resale liquidity or a track record of secondary-market price appreciation — Shurooq's core competency to date has been institutional master-planning and destination-building, not repeat residential resale performance.

The Project, in Figures

  • Data185 residential units across 6 residential buildings

The development sits on Khorfakkan's East Coast, facing the Gulf of Oman with the Hajar Mountains behind it — a dual-aspect site that is genuinely uncommon in the UAE, where beachfront and mountain-view stock are almost never the same address.

A considered read: Ajwan is one project inside a government authority's wider portfolio, not a standalone developer's flagship. That reduces single-project delivery risk relative to a private off-plan sponsor, but it also means resale demand depends on Khorfakkan establishing itself as a destination — a process still in progress, not yet proven at scale.

Phasing and Delivery

The master plan is structured across named residential clusters — Saahil, Mawj, Ghadeer, Al Joon, and, added in the most recent release, Layan and Juman — arranged around a private marina, beach frontage, retail, and a planned hotel component. Phase 3, launched in December 2025, introduced the Layan and Juman towers: Layan fronts the seafront boardwalk and retail area, Juman fronts the marina. Shurooq's Chief Real Estate Officer, Yousif Ahmed Al Mutawa, described the phase as combining contemporary design with the site's natural surroundings.

Construction across all phases now sits with a single main contractor, Darwish Engineering Emirates, appointed at the Phase 3 launch — a consolidation that is a genuine positive signal for delivery consistency, replacing what would otherwise be a phase-by-phase contractor risk. Shurooq reports that Phases 1 and 2 (Al Joon, Saahil, Mawj, Ghadeer) have already recorded strong sales performance, though unit-level absorption rates and secondary pricing are not independently published.

What the Individual Buildings Reveal About Pricing

Shurooq does not publish a single blended price list for Ajwan — pricing is set building by building, and the spread between them is wide enough to be a market signal in itself:

  • Market claimGhadeer (5 floors, 24 units) — 2-bedroom units from AED 2.0M, 3-bedroom from AED 3.0M–3.7M
  • Market claimSaahil (8 floors, 39 units, boardwalk-facing) — 3-bedroom duplexes from AED 4.1M, 4-bedroom duplexes from AED 4.9M–5.1M
  • Market claimMawj (5 floors, 27 units, private beach access) — 3-bedroom from AED 4.1M, 4-bedroom duplexes from AED 5.4M

The gap is not incidental. Saahil and Mawj — the buildings with direct beach access and boardwalk frontage — carry a duplex entry price roughly 40–50% above Ghadeer's comparable stock, despite all three sitting inside the same 89,100 sqm master plan. That is a genuine amenity-and-frontage premium being priced by the developer itself, not a broker narrative, and it means "Ajwan pricing" as a single figure is close to meaningless — the building matters as much as the project. Per-sqft pricing across the disclosed units clusters in a wide AED 1,300–1,900/sqft range, well above the blended AED 900–1,330/sqft estimate a size-only calculation would suggest, because the highest-value duplex stock skews the disclosed figures upward. Pricing for Al Joon, Layan and Juman had not been separately published in the developer's own building pages at the time of writing.

Why Khorfakkan, and Why Now

Market claim The site case rests on connectivity, not just scenery. A decade of infrastructure investment has brought Khorfakkan within roughly 90 minutes of both Dubai and Sharjah city — recasting a historically remote East Coast town as a viable second-home and weekend-retreat market rather than a purely destination-holiday one. Sharjah's real estate transaction value reached $17.86 billion (AED 65.6 billion) in 2025, up 64.3% year-on-year, with AED 44.3 billion ($12.1bn) transacted in the first nine months of 2025 alone — a 58.3% increase on the same period in 2024. During Ramadan 2026, transaction value reportedly rose 71.8% year-on-year to $1.25 billion. These are Shurooq/developer-sourced figures rather than independently audited DLD-equivalent data.

Longer-term site works described by Shurooq include forestation around Khorfakkan to increase oxygen levels and reduce emissions, rainwater harvesting and self-sustaining irrigation, and mountain-integrated housing with elevated road networks reaching up to 1,100 metres in altitude — ambitious infrastructure that, if delivered, would materially differentiate the destination, but is at an early or planning stage rather than complete.

Amenities and Positioning

The master plan is anchored around a private marina, dedicated beach access, a waterpark, swimming pools, landscaped grounds, retail and F&B, and a planned hotel — positioning Ajwan as an integrated resort-residential community rather than a standalone apartment block. This amenity stack is competitive with comparable branded coastal launches elsewhere in the UAE, though Ajwan itself carries no international hospitality brand attached to the residences — the value proposition rests on the master developer's institutional standing and the site's dual mountain-and-sea aspect, not on a branded-residence premium.

The Risk Worth Sitting With

Two considerations are genuinely open rather than resolved:

  • Destination maturity. Khorfakkan is still mid-transformation. The forestation, elevated road network, and hotel component are stated ambitions rather than delivered infrastructure. Ajwan's resale value depends materially on these wider plans landing on schedule, not solely on the residential towers themselves.
  • Absence of independent pricing data. Unlike Dubai's DLD-published transaction registry, comparable independently verified per-sqft pricing and secondary-market transaction data for Khorfakkan are not readily available in public sources at the time of writing. The Sharjah Real Estate Registration Department (SRERD) publishes aggregated regional transaction volume — Khorfakkan recorded AED 262.4 million in transactions across Q1–Q3 — but not unit-level or project-level pricing. Treat the per-sqft and absorption figures above as the developer/broker's own market narrative: directionally useful, but not a registry-confirmed comparable, and worth verifying directly with Shurooq's sales office before committing.

Our Assessment

For mandates seeking government-backed delivery risk (rather than private-developer counterparty risk) in an emerging, non-Dubai UAE coastal market, Ajwan Khorfakkan is a credible entry point — a real, phased, single-contractor project inside a sovereign developer's broader and demonstrably growing portfolio. It is not, at this stage, a liquid or price-proven secondary market in the way Dubai Marina or Al Marjan Island now are. The appropriate posture is destination-conviction with a longer hold horizon, not a short-cycle appreciation play — and, given the building-by-building price spread above, a mandate that starts with which building fits the brief, not the project as a whole.

Frequently Asked

Who is developing Ajwan Khorfakkan?

The Sharjah Investment and Development Authority (Shurooq), Sharjah's government investment and development authority, which also developed Maryam Island and Sharjah Sustainable City.

How large is Ajwan Khorfakkan and how many units does it have?

89,100 sqm of total land with a 33,900 sqm residences area, across six residential buildings and 185 apartments in 2- to 4-bedroom layouts and duplexes.

What is included in Phase 3?

Phase 3, launched December 2025, introduced the Layan and Juman towers, alongside the appointment of Darwish Engineering Emirates as main contractor across all phases of the project.

Why does pricing vary so much across Ajwan's buildings?

Beachfront and boardwalk-facing buildings (Saahil, Mawj) carry a materially higher entry price than inland-facing buildings (Ghadeer) for comparable unit types — a frontage and amenity premium the developer itself prices, not a broker markup.

How far is Khorfakkan from Dubai?

Approximately 90 minutes by road, following a decade of connectivity and infrastructure investment cited by Shurooq.
