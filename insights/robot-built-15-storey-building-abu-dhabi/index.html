A 15-Storey Building in 12 Days. Read It Carefully.

Eagle Hills assembled a 15-storey modular building in Abu Dhabi in 12 days. Here is what it could mean for developers, investors and off-plan buyers.

Home / Insights / Robot-built 15-storey tower

By Navvin S Bhalla · Zen Homes Advisory — Last updated November 8, 2026

Share Copied

In short: Mohamed Alabbar, Founder and Chairman of Eagle Hills, says his group built a 15-storey modular building in China using robots, shipped it in containers, and assembled it in Abu Dhabi in 12 days. If the performance claims hold, delivery speed and build quality could change how UAE projects are timed, priced and valued. It remains a pilot, and investors should treat it as a signal to watch, not a settled trend.

What Was Announced

Speaking at the Adria Future Summit 2026 in Porto Montenegro (22–24 April), Alabbar, who also founded Emaar, described a pilot project: a 15-storey building manufactured by robots in China using Broad Group's Holon modular system, shipped in containers, and assembled on site in Abu Dhabi in 12 days. The project was first publicly highlighted in December 2025.

His stated performance claims, which are his own and have not been independently verified by Zen Homes:

  • About 90% energy efficiency
  • 100% better indoor air quality
  • Roughly 10 times greater earthquake resistance

Why Investors Should Care

  • Time to market. Off-plan buyers carry delivery risk, and long build cycles tie up capital. Faster assembly could shorten the gap between purchase and handover.
  • Quality consistency. Factory production allows tighter quality control and less weather exposure than traditional site-built construction.
  • Running costs. If the energy-efficiency claims are accurate, lower operating costs could support net yields and tenant demand, in a market where service charges directly affect returns.
  • Supply dynamics. Faster delivery could increase the speed at which supply reaches some segments. Investors should watch how that affects pricing and rents rather than assume it helps or hurts.

What We Still Don't Know

  • Whether the performance claims survive independent testing
  • Whether a pilot can scale to full communities
  • How regulators, lenders and insurers will treat modular buildings
  • How the resale market will value them against conventional stock

Our View

For NRI and international investors, the practical takeaway is not to chase the headline but to ask sharper questions. On any off-plan purchase, ask what the construction method is, who certifies its performance, and how the developer manages delivery risk. Modular building may become part of the answer, but it is early.

Source: Mohamed Alabbar, Adria Future Summit 2026. Performance figures are speaker claims, not verified data.
