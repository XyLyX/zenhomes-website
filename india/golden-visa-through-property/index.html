How to Get the UAE Golden Visa Through Property Investment

The complete process for turning an AED 2 million+ Dubai property purchase into a 10-year UAE Golden Visa — from valuation to Emirates ID.

Home / India & NRI Investors / Golden Visa Through Property

By Navvin S Bhalla · Zen Homes Advisory — Last updated August 28, 2026

Once an investor has decided to pursue the Golden Visa rather than the lower-tier 2-year property visa, the question stops being "am I eligible" and becomes "what do I actually do." This is a process guide: the sequence of steps, the authority you deal with at each stage, the documents that get requested, and the fees and timelines currently published by the Dubai Land Department (DLD) and the General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs (GDRFA), so an investor coordinating this from India — often through a power of attorney and a local advisor — knows exactly what to expect and when.

Step-by-Step Application Process

The property-investor Golden Visa runs through two connected government bodies, and it helps to know the handoff before you start.

DataWhich authority handles it: The Dubai Land Department operates a dedicated Golden Visa nomination service for investors — DLD verifies the property ownership and value, then submits the nomination to the Federal Authority for Identity, Citizenship, Customs & Port Security (ICP) on the applicant's behalf. GDRFA separately runs its own "Real Estate Investor" residence-permit service for Dubai. Most property-only applicants go through DLD's nomination route since it is purpose-built for this category and coordinates the ICP handoff directly.

  1. Complete the property purchase and title registration. The property must be registered with DLD (title deed, or an Oqood certificate for off-plan) with a recorded value of at least AED 2,000,000.
  2. Confirm eligibility before applying. Verify your registered ownership and value through the Dubai REST app (using UAE Pass) or at a DLD service center — this also confirms whether combined values across multiple properties clear the threshold.
  3. Book and attend a DLD Golden Visa service center appointment. DLD's own service centers include Al Manara Center (Dubai Land Department "Cube"), Dubai World Trade Centre, and authorized registration trustees.
  4. Submit documents and pay fees. Passport copy, title deed / e-Certificate of Title, a personal photograph, existing UAE Emirates ID (if applicable), and current residence permit copy (if applicable) — plus attested marriage/birth certificates for any family being sponsored at the same time.
  5. Complete the medical fitness test. Done at a DHA-approved medical center — blood screening and a chest X-ray are standard; this is a mandatory step before Emirates ID issuance.
  6. Emirates ID biometrics. Fingerprints and photo captured at an ICP-linked service center once the medical clearance comes through.
  7. Permit issuance. DLD states the completed 10-year Golden Visa is issued and sent by email within 7–10 business days of submission.

Market claim"You apply for the Golden Visa the same way you'd apply for any UAE residence visa." In practice the investor route is a nomination process, not a direct immigration application — DLD verifies the property and forwards your file to ICP. Applicants don't file with ICP or GDRFA independently; the property authority is the front door.

Timeline & Costs

DataDLD's official fee schedule for the primary applicant: a total of AED 9,884.75, broken down as medical examination AED 700, Emirates ID AED 1,153, residency confirmation AED 2,856.75, DLD nomination fee AED 4,020, and administrative charges AED 1,155.

DataGDRFA's separate investor-category fee: the base 10-year residence permit fee is AED 1,100, plus Knowledge and Innovation Dirham charges of AED 10 each, a domestic fee of AED 500, and a AED 20 delivery fee — with a roughly AED 100 annual increase after the first two years. Note this GDRFA figure covers the residence permit only; medical testing and Emirates ID are billed separately under that channel, so it isn't directly comparable to DLD's all-in total above.

DataProcessing time: DLD quotes 7–10 business days from document submission to permit issuance; GDRFA quotes an expected 5 days for its investor category, though both note additional approvals can extend this. Industry advisors report a realistic end-to-end window of roughly 2–4 weeks once the medical test, Emirates ID processing, and visa stamping are all accounted for — this excludes however long the property purchase and title registration itself takes.

Family sponsorship is billed per person, separately from the primary applicant's fees — see below.

Family Sponsorship

The property-based Golden Visa carries meaningfully broader sponsorship rights than a standard UAE residence visa.

DataSpouse: sponsored for a matching 10-year residence permit with an attested marriage certificate, passport copy, photo, and active health insurance.

Market claim"Sons lose sponsorship eligibility at 18, same as a regular visa." Not on the Golden Visa. The age cutoffs that apply to standard UAE dependent visas are removed for this category — sons can remain sponsored regardless of age, and unmarried daughters qualify at any age, provided the relevant supporting documents (attested birth certificates, insurance) are in order.

DataParents: can be sponsored without the salary or income thresholds that apply to standard UAE dependent-visa sponsorship, using dependency certificates from the parents' home-country consulate plus attested birth certificates and health insurance.

DataDomestic staff: Golden Visa holders can sponsor domestic staff — nannies, housekeepers, drivers, private chefs — without the salary restrictions that apply to staff sponsorship under a standard residence visa.

DataDLD fees per dependent: AED 5,774.50 per family member sponsored, plus a one-time AED 318.75 file-opening fee; parent sponsorship is billed at the same AED 5,774.50 per person.

Renewal & What Happens If You Sell

DataRenewal: the visa is renewable indefinitely in 10-year cycles, but renewal is conditional — you must resubmit an updated title deed, current property valuation, valid Emirates ID, and active health insurance showing you still hold qualifying real estate worth at least AED 2,000,000 at the time of renewal.

Market claim"If I sell the property at any point, my Golden Visa is cancelled immediately." Not quite. Selling mid-term doesn't retroactively void a visa that's already active. What it does trigger is a block at the point of transfer: DLD will not process the sale/transfer of the underlying title unless one of three conditions is met first — you can show other qualifying property in your name worth AED 2,000,000+, you transfer the investment into a new qualifying property before the sale completes, or you formally cancel the Golden Visa ahead of the transfer. In other words, the constraint isn't "never sell" — it's "don't try to sell out from under the visa without one of these three steps first."

Off-Plan and Multiple-Property Eligibility

DataOff-plan qualifies before completion. An off-plan purchase from a RERA-registered developer, registered with DLD via an Oqood certificate, is eligible for the Golden Visa nomination without waiting for handover — provided the property's total value is AED 2,000,000 or more. If the full purchase price hasn't been paid yet, the paid-up portion must independently meet the AED 2,000,000 threshold; a developer NOC is typically required as part of the application file.

Market claim"Off-plan doesn't count until you have the final title deed." This is the most common misunderstanding investors bring to us. The Oqood interim registration is itself sufficient for the nomination — the handover/final title deed is not a prerequisite, only a correctly registered Oqood at the required value is.

DataMultiple properties can be combined. The AED 2,000,000 threshold does not require a single property — values of multiple properties registered under the same individual's name, within eligible freehold/investment zones, can be aggregated to clear the threshold. Eligibility across combined holdings can be checked directly through the Dubai REST app.

DataMortgaged property is eligible. Where a property is financed, it is generally the paid-up equity — not the total property value — that has to clear the threshold, supported by a bank confirmation letter or NOC from a UAE-licensed lender; industry guidance for 2025–2026 indicates there is no separate minimum down-payment percentage imposed purely for Golden Visa purposes beyond meeting that equity threshold. Confirm current bank-specific NOC requirements at the time of application, since lender practice varies.

Frequently Asked

Can I apply for the Golden Visa before my off-plan property is completed?

Yes. Once your off-plan purchase is registered with the Dubai Land Department via an Oqood certificate and the recorded (or paid-up) value meets AED 2,000,000, you can apply for the nomination without waiting for handover — you do not need the final title deed to start the process.

What happens to my Golden Visa if I sell the property before the 10 years is up?

The visa isn't automatically cancelled by an earlier sale, but DLD will block the transfer at closing unless you either hold other qualifying property worth AED 2,000,000+, roll the investment into a new qualifying property before the sale completes, or formally cancel the visa ahead of the transfer.

Can I combine two smaller apartments to reach the AED 2 million Golden Visa threshold?

Yes. Multiple properties registered under your name in eligible freehold zones can be combined to meet the AED 2,000,000 threshold — you can verify your combined qualifying value through the Dubai REST app before applying.
